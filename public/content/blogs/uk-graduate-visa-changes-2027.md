## UK Graduate Visa Changes in 2027

Planning to study in the UK and wondering what happens after graduation? The UK Graduate route is changing in 2027, and the change is important for international students.

From 1 January 2027, eligible graduates of bachelor’s, master’s and other relevant non-doctoral qualifications will receive 18 months of Graduate permission when they apply on or after that date, instead of the current 2 years.

The 3-year period for people who have completed a PhD or another doctoral qualification is not being reduced.

Because the date of application determines the period granted, students should understand the new rule before planning their studies and post-study career.

## What Is the UK Graduate Visa?

The Graduate route gives eligible international students a period of stay in the UK after successfully completing an eligible course. It is an unsponsored route, so you do not need a sponsored job to apply for it.

Depending on the rules and your circumstances, a Graduate visa can allow you to work, look for work, be self-employed and undertake permitted study. You cannot work as a professional sportsperson.

The Graduate route is temporary and is not itself a settlement route.

## What Is Changing From 1 January 2027?

The main change is the length of the Graduate permission granted to eligible non-doctoral graduates.

| Qualification | Application before 1 Jan 2027 | Application on/after 1 Jan 2027 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bachelor’s / Master’s / other relevant non-doctoral qualification | 2 years | 18 months |
| PhD / other doctoral qualification | 3 years | 3 years |


The official Immigration Rules specify that eligible non-doctoral graduates can receive 2 years when the application is made before 1 January 2027 and 18 months when the application is made on or after 1 January 2027. PhD and other doctoral qualification graduates continue to receive 3 years.

## The Important Date Students Need to Remember

**1 January 2027** is the key date for the new Graduate route duration.

Application made before **1 January 2027 → 2 years** for eligible non **doctoral qualifications**.

Application made on or after **1 January 2027 → 18 months** for eligible non **doctoral qualifications**.

**PhD** or another **doctoral qualification → 3 years**.

This means the date you submit your **Graduate route** application matters. It is not simply the year in which you started or completed your course.

## Why Is the UK Changing the Graduate Route?

The UK Government announced the reduction as part of wider changes to the immigration system. The Government’s explanatory memorandum states that the Graduate route will be reduced to 18 months for applications submitted from 1 January 2027, while PhD graduates will continue to receive three years.

For students, the practical takeaway is simple: the post-study period for most eligible non-doctoral graduates will be shorter, so career planning should begin well before graduation.

## What Can You Do on a Graduate Visa?

- Work in the UK.
- Look for employment.
- Be self-employed.
- Undertake permitted study.
- Carry out voluntary work.

The Graduate route does not permit work as a professional sportsperson.

If you want to remain in the UK after your Graduate permission ends, you may need to move to another immigration route if you meet its requirements. For example, some graduates may later qualify for the Skilled Worker route.

## Can the Graduate Visa Be Extended?

No. The Graduate visa **cannot be extended**. However, you may be able to switch to another immigration route before your current permission expires, provided you satisfy that route’s requirements.

## Who Can Apply for the Graduate Visa?

The **Graduate route** has detailed eligibility requirements. Broadly, an applicant must:

- Be in the UK when applying.
- Have, or have last held, permission as a Student.
- Have successfully completed an eligible course in the UK.
- Meet the required study-period conditions.
- Meet the relevant validity, suitability and other requirements in the Immigration Rules.

There are additional requirements and exceptions for some students, including people whose studies were sponsored by a government or international scholarship agency. Eligibility should therefore be checked against the rules that apply to the individual case.

## When Should Students Apply?

A **Graduate route** application must be made from **inside the UK** and before the applicant’s current Student permission expires.
Students should not assume that the course completion date alone determines the Graduate visa duration. For the 2027 change, the application date is particularly important.

- Apply before **1 January 2027 → 2 years** for an eligible non-**doctoral qualification**.
- Apply on or after **1 January 2027 → 18 months** for an eligible non-**doctoral qualification**.
- **PhD** or other **doctoral qualification → 3 years**.

## What Does the 18-Month Rule Mean for International Students?

The shorter Graduate period does not mean international students cannot work in the UK after graduation. It means eligible non-doctoral graduates will have less time under this particular route.

- Students who want to build a longer-term career in the UK should therefore think about their next steps early.
- Choose a course that fits your career goals.
- Build relevant skills and experience while studying.
- Start exploring suitable graduate jobs before finishing your course.
- Understand the requirements of possible future visa routes.
- Keep your immigration documents and expiry dates organised.

## Does the Change Affect PhD Students?

No. The 3-year **Graduate route** period remains for people who have successfully completed a PhD or another doctoral qualification. The 18-month change applies to eligible non-**doctoral qualifications**.

## What About Dependants?

The **Graduate route** has specific rules for dependants. A partner or child generally needs to meet the requirements for a Graduate dependant, including rules concerning their previous permission as the dependant of a Student. There are limited exceptions, including certain children born in the UK during the relevant Student permission.

Therefore, students should not assume that a new partner or child can automatically join them from overseas under the **Graduate route**. The current Immigration Rules should be checked for the exact circumstances.

## How Much Does the Graduate Visa Cost?

**As of 8 August 2026**, GOV.UK lists a **£937** application fee for the **Graduate route**. Applicants must also pay the Immigration Health Surcharge.

The current GOV.UK guidance lists these healthcare surcharge amounts:

- 18-month Graduate visa: £1,152.50
- 2-year Graduate visa: £2,070
- 3-year Graduate visa: £3,105

Fees can change. Applicants should confirm the amount shown by GOV.UK when they are ready to apply.

## Graduate Visa vs Long-Term Career Planning

The **Graduate route** can provide valuable time to gain UK work experience, but it should not be treated as a guarantee of long-term residence.

If a student wants to stay in the UK beyond the **Graduate route**, they should research the requirements of other routes early. A possible next step for some graduates is employer sponsorship under the **Skilled Worker** route, but eligibility depends on the job, employer and applicant meeting the relevant rules.

Professional advice may be appropriate for complex immigration situations.

## Key Takeaway

The UK Graduate Visa is not being abolished. The important change is that, from 1 January 2027, eligible non-doctoral graduates applying from that date will receive 18 months rather than 2 years. PhD and other doctoral qualification graduates will continue to receive 3 years.

For students planning to study in the UK, this makes it more important to combine course planning with career planning. Understanding the post-study options before you start your degree can help you make a more informed decision.

## Final Thoughts
The UK remains an attractive destination for international education, but immigration rules can change. If you are considering a UK degree, look beyond the course itself and think about your career goals, work opportunities and possible post-study immigration options.

## FAQs
**1. Will the UK Graduate Visa become 18 months in 2027?**

   Yes. For eligible non-doctoral qualifications, applications made on or after 1 January 2027 will receive 18 months rather than 2 years.

**2. Will PhD graduates also get only 18 months?**

   No. Graduates who have completed a PhD or another doctoral qualification will continue to receive 3 years.

**3. What happens if I apply before 1 January 2027?**

   If you make an eligible Graduate route application before 1 January 2027, the applicable period for eligible non-doctoral qualifications is 2 years.

**4. Does my graduation date decide whether I get 18 months?**

   Not by itself. For the 2027 duration change, the date of the Graduate route application is important.

**5. Can I extend my Graduate Visa?**

   No. The Graduate route cannot be extended. You may be able to switch to another route if you meet its requirements.

**6. Can I apply for the Graduate Visa from India?**

   No. The Graduate route application must be made from inside the UK.

**7. Can my spouse or children come with me on the Graduate route?**

   Only if they meet the specific dependant requirements. The rules generally require them to have already held dependant permission linked to the Student route, subject to limited exceptions.

**8. Does the Graduate Visa give permanent residency automatically?**

   No. The Graduate route is not a settlement route. A person who wants to remain longer may need to qualify for another route.
