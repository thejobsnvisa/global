For most eligible international graduates, the post-study stay will reduce from **2 years to 18 months** for Graduate visa applications made on or after **1 January 2027**.

However, graduates who complete a PhD or another doctoral qualification will continue to receive a **3-year Graduate visa**.

If you are planning to study in the UK, understanding this change can help you plan your studies and career more effectively. 

## What Is the UK Graduate Visa?

The Graduate visa allows eligible international students to stay in the UK after successfully completing an eligible course. 

It gives graduates time to work, look for work or gain professional experience in the UK. 

The visa is generally available to students who have successfully completed an eligible course while holding the required Student visa or other relevant permission. 

The Graduate visa is a **post-study work route**. It is separate from the Student visa and is designed for eligible graduates who want to remain in the UK after their studies. 

## What Is Changing From 2027?

The main change is the length of permission granted under the Graduate route. 

### If you apply on or before 31 December 2026

Eligible graduates who apply for the Graduate visa on or before 31 December 2026 can generally receive: 

**2 years of permission**

## If you apply on or after 1 January 2027

Eligible graduates applying on or after 1 January 2027 will generally receive: 

**18 months of permission**

## PhD and doctoral graduates

There is an important exception. 

If you have successfully completed a **PhD or another doctoral qualification**, you can continue to receive: 

**3 years of Graduate visa permission**

The UK Government has confirmed that the 18-month change applies to applications submitted from 1 January 2027, while doctoral graduates continue to receive three years.

The key point is that the **Graduate visa application** date determines whether the 2-year or 18-month period applies to eligible non-doctoral graduates. 

## Is the UK Graduate Visa Being Removed?

**No.** 

The Graduate visa is not being removed. 

The confirmed change is the reduction in the length of the visa for most eligible non-doctoral graduates. 

The Graduate route will continue to be available to people who meet its requirements. 

## Who Will Be Affected by the 2027 Change?

The shorter 18-month period will apply to eligible graduates who apply for the Graduate visa from **1 January** 2027 and who do not qualify for the three-year doctoral period. 

This can include eligible graduates who have completed qualifying bachelor’s or master’s-level courses. 

The exact eligibility requirements still need to be met, including completing an eligible course and satisfying the other Graduate route requirements. 

Therefore, students should not assume that simply completing a UK degree automatically guarantees a Graduate visa.

## Can Graduate Visa Holders Work in the UK?

Yes. 

The Graduate visa allows eligible holders to work in the UK, including in most jobs. 

It can also allow graduates to look for work and gain professional experience after completing their studies. 

However, the Graduate visa does **not guarantee employment**. Students are responsible for finding suitable work or other opportunities during their permission period. 

## Can You Switch From a Graduate Visa to a Skilled Worker Visa?

A Graduate visa cannot be extended. 

However, a Graduate visa holder may be able to **switch to another eligible immigration route**, such as the Skilled Worker route, if they meet all the requirements for that route. 

This can include requirements relating to the job, employer, sponsorship and salary. 

The Skilled Worker requirements can change, so graduates should check the latest GOV.UK rules before applying.

## What About PhD Students?

PhD and other doctoral graduates are treated differently under the Graduate route. 

The 2027 change does **not reduce their Graduate visa period to 18 months**. 

Eligible doctoral graduates continue to receive 3 years of permission. 

## Can Graduate Visa Holders Bring Dependants?

Dependants are subject to specific rules. 

A partner or child who already has permission as a dependant may be able to apply to remain as a dependant of a Graduate visa holder, subject to the relevant requirements. 

However,**new dependants generally cannot simply join a Graduate visa holder under the route**. There are specific exceptions, including certain children born in the UK. 

Because dependant eligibility depends on individual circumstances, students should check the current GOV.UK guidance before making plans.
